JUMPjet Support

Just-in-time Unified Modelling of Protein Jumps, Ensembles and Transitions

Type a UniProt accession, watch the protein move. JUMPjet fetches a structure, runs a torsional Monte Carlo sampler on your device, and shows you which parts of the molecule are mobile: as a 3D animation you can scrub, as per-residue analytics, and as a movie you can export.

Getting started

Enter a UniProt accession such as P69905 (human haemoglobin alpha) or P00698 (hen lysozyme), or a PDB entry such as 1BAB. Press Run. A few thousand sweeps take seconds to a minute depending on the size of the protein and your device. Then scrub the timeline, open the flight recorder for per-residue detail, or export a movie.

What the numbers mean, and what they do not

This matters more than anything else on this page, so it is stated plainly here and in the app itself:

Frames are Monte Carlo sweeps, not femtoseconds. JUMPjet is not a molecular dynamics engine and does not compute a trajectory in time. There is no timestep, and no frame count corresponds to any number of nanoseconds.

Jump rates reflect the sampler's move set as much as the protein. The sampler proposes rotamer jumps at a fixed rate, so the number of jumps a residue makes is partly a property of how the sampler was configured. Rank residues against each other within one run; do not compare rates between runs, and do not read them as kinetics.

JUMPjet is for building intuition about which parts of a structure are mobile. It is not for computing thermodynamics or kinetics, and it does not claim to be.

Common questions

QuestionAnswer
Does it need an internet connection?Only to fetch a structure. Once one is loaded, sampling, analysis and export are entirely on device, and airplane mode changes nothing.
An accession will not load.Check it is a UniProt accession (like P69905) rather than a gene name or an entry name. If the AlphaFold database has no model for it, JUMPjet falls back to an experimental structure through PDBe, and some entries have neither.
A large protein is slow.Cost grows with the number of residues. A few hundred residues is comfortable on a recent device; several thousand will be slow. Reduce the sweep count for a first look.
Where do the structures come from?AlphaFold DB and UniProtKB, with experimental structures from PDBe. Attribution and licences are listed in the repository.
Is there a Mac version?Not currently. JUMPjet is iPhone and iPad only, because the sampler was tuned and measured for a phone's thermal envelope.

Reporting a problem

The fastest route is a GitHub issue at bellcheddar/JUMPjet, which is also where the source lives. If you would rather not use GitHub, email marc@marcdeller.com. Please say which device and iOS version you are on, and which accession you were loading.
